**Short answer:** a managerial accounting course is mostly calculation problems (cost behavior, cost-volume-profit, job and process costing, budgets, variances) plus decision cases such as make-or-buy or special orders. An AI tool will produce a full worked answer to most of these, with numbers that may or may not be right. Your policy should say which practice students must do themselves, how they show the calculation behind a recommendation, and whether checking AI output is itself an assignment. Pick one of the three policies below, then add the clauses that fit your course.

## What makes managerial accounting different

- **The work is calculation feeding a decision.** Students compute a contribution margin, a predetermined overhead rate or a variance, then say what a manager should do. A wrong number early in the chain leads to a confident but wrong recommendation, and a final answer alone does not show where the reasoning went.
- **AI has been weaker here than on other accounting topics, but do not count on it.** A large 2023 study gave the original ChatGPT more than 25,000 accounting exam questions from 186 institutions. Its lowest scores were in financial, managerial and tax accounting, and it beat the student average on only 3.8% of managerial assessments; the authors suggest the math in these topics may be one reason ([Wood et al., 2023](https://aaahq.org/portals/0/documents/publications/issues-2023-013.pdf)). That model is now well out of date, so treat this as a floor, not a guide to current tools.
- **Decision cases lend themselves to checking AI output.** Because a make-or-buy or special-order analysis has a right set of relevant costs, students can recompute an AI answer and find its errors. One cost accounting course built an assignment around exactly this ([Wiseman and Foster, 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0748575125000399)).

## Three sample policies

Copy the one closest to your course and edit the bracketed parts.

### 1. No generative AI on graded work

> Generative AI tools (for example ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot) may not be used on homework, quizzes, cases or exams in this course. Working through cost calculations, budgets and variance analyses yourself is how you learn to support a business decision with numbers, and these tools skip that practice. You may use the textbook, lecture notes, [the online homework system's hints], [a spreadsheet], classmates [if allowed] and office hours. If you are unsure whether something is allowed, ask before you submit.

### 2. AI for studying, not for submitted work

> You may use generative AI tools to study: to explain a concept such as fixed versus variable costs or absorption versus variable costing, to create extra practice problems, or to check your understanding after you have attempted a problem. You may not use them to produce calculations, schedules, budgets or written recommendations that you submit. Your submitted work should show each step: the cost classification, the formula, the amounts and the conclusion. If you used an AI tool while studying for an assignment, add one sentence at the end saying how.

### 3. AI allowed with disclosure

> On [homework / the case project], you may use generative AI tools if you disclose it. For each assignment, list the tool, what you used it for and the key prompts. You are responsible for every figure you submit: recompute any number an AI tool gives you and check that you used only the relevant costs. Written recommendations must be in your own words. AI use is not allowed on [quizzes / exams], which are where your understanding is assessed.

## Clauses worth adding for managerial accounting

- **Show the relevant costs.** "For any decision problem, list the costs and revenues you treated as relevant and the ones you excluded, with a short reason. Answers without this will not receive full credit, whether or not AI was used."
- **Recompute, then critique.** If you want students to work with AI, assign it directly: give them an AI-generated analysis of a case and grade their recalculation and the errors they find. The Wiseman and Foster assignment used a make-or-buy scenario this way, and their evidence is a survey of 39 students at one university, so it shows students liked it rather than that it improved learning.
- **Spreadsheets versus AI chatbots.** If students build budgets or CVP models in a spreadsheet, say whether AI features inside the spreadsheet count as AI use under your policy.
- **What to disclose.** Name the tool, what it was used for and the prompts that shaped the answer ([KU CTE](https://cte.ku.edu/building-ai-policies-your-syllabus)).
